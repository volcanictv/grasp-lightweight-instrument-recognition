\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Surgical instrument segmentation and recognition.}
ISINet \citep{gonzalez2020isinet} treats instrument segmentation as instance segmentation with a temporal consistency module, and MATIS \citep{ayobi2023matis} replaces the convolutional backbone with masked-attention transformers and adds a video transformer for the class.
The GraSP benchmark and its TAPIS model \citep{ayobi2024pixel} extend this line to a holistic, multi-task setting: a Mask2Former detector \citep{cheng2022mask2former} proposes instrument regions and a video transformer classifies them from a clip around the keyframe.
Text-promptable segmentation \citep{zhou2023tpsis} conditions the mask decoder on instrument names so that new instrument types can be handled without relabelling.
These methods detect, segment and classify jointly, read a window of frames around the keyframe, and apply the same temporal context to every instrument; to our knowledge none of them uses an uncertainty estimate to decide where temporal context is needed.

\paragraph{Promptable and foundation segmentation.}
SAM \citep{kirillov2023sam} introduced segmentation from points and boxes, MedSAM \citep{ma2024medsam} adapted it to medical images, and SAM2 \citep{ravi2024sam2} added propagation of a mask through video.
SAM3 \citep{carion2025sam3} adds concept prompts.
In surgery these models are used zero-shot or lightly fine-tuned; recent work from our group prompts SAM3 with a generic text prompt and classifies the masked regions with a vision-language model \citep{poudel2026sam3}.
We instead give the model a box, fine-tune its decoder (and, for SAM2, its last encoder blocks) on the benchmark, and leave classification to a dedicated light classifier, which is what lets us attach an uncertainty to it.
EdgeTAM \citep{zhou2025edgetam} is a lighter tracker in the same family, whose crops we use to train the classifier.

\paragraph{Uncertainty.}
MC dropout \citep{gal2016dropout} estimates uncertainty from many stochastic forward passes and softmax confidence is the usual cheap baseline; both are poorly calibrated under dataset shift \citep{guo2017calibration,ovadia2019trust}.
Evidential deep learning \citep{sensoy2018evidential} places a Dirichlet distribution over the class probabilities and obtains a belief and its uncertainty in one pass; \citet{duan2024evidential} derive variance-based scores that separate epistemic from aleatoric uncertainty, and we use their epistemic score as the gate.
Unlike a vote or a softmax, such a score can stay high when all ensemble members agree on the wrong class, because it also reflects how much evidence the members gathered.

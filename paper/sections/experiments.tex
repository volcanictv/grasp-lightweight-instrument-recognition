\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
We use the GraSP benchmark (robotic prostatectomy, 7 instrument classes, frames at one per second).
The official test split has 1,125 frames from 5 cases with 2,861 instruments; two disjoint folds of the training cases are the development folds, and the final models are trained on all eight training cases.
We report the benchmark's mIoU, IoU and mcIoU, computed with code ported from the MATIS evaluation, and bootstrap intervals over the five test cases for the headline scores.
The instruments' boxes are the ground-truth boxes: the pipeline recognises given instruments and assumes that localisation is provided.
Numbers are the mean of three classifier seeds; each segmenter was trained once.

The gate threshold is chosen on a held-out fold.
The weight 0.40 of the first ensemble member and the 29\% operating point used to read the effort curve were set while looking at test scores in earlier iterations, so the main results use the held-out threshold and the configuration with the test-selected share is in Appendix~\ref{app:extra}.

Figure~\ref{fig:effort}a ranks the instruments by uncertainty and shows how many of the errors the highest-ranked contain.
Reviewing 20\% of the instruments finds 90.2\% of the errors on the held-out fold (against 86.4\% for softmax confidence, 83.9\% for an MC-dropout vote and 88.9\% for a deep ensemble of three seeds) and 84.8\% on the test cases, where the AUROC is 0.922.

The curves lie close together, as the AUROCs of Table~\ref{tab:uncertainty} show; the evidential score leads at the point where a reviewer would stop.

Figure~\ref{fig:effort}b shows the same ranking used for refinement.
Refining the 19\% with the weakest evidence raises instrument accuracy from 0.909 to 0.948, which is 81\% of the gain reached at 31\% (0.957); refining 29\% recovers 97\% of the best gain we measured, which is reached at 42\% (0.958).
A gate that knew which instruments refinement would fix would reach 0.970 on the same instruments, so the evidence captures most of what a gate can capture but not all of it.
On one A100 a frame with SAM2 and SAM3 masks and the four-member classification takes 1.2\,s and each refined instrument a further 6.1\,s, so refining 29\% gives 3.0\,s per instrument on average against an estimated 7.3\,s if every instrument were refined.

\label{sec:uncertainty}
\begin{table}[tbp]
\floatconts
  {tab:uncertainty}
  {\caption{Calibration (ECE with 15 bins; NLL) and error detection (AUROC; share of errors found when 20\% of the instruments are reviewed) on the held-out fold, mean of three seeds (one run for the 12-network rows). Nets: networks run per instrument.}}
  {\footnotesize\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}
  \toprule
  \bfseries Method and score & \bfseries Nets & \bfseries ECE & \bfseries NLL & \bfseries AUROC & \bfseries Found (\%)\\
  \midrule
  Softmax ensemble, max-softmax & 4 & 0.027 & 0.221 & 0.926 & 86.4\\
  Softmax ensemble, energy & 4 & 0.027 & 0.221 & 0.907 & 80.7\\
  Softmax, temperature scaled & 4 & 0.036 & 0.218 & 0.930 & 87.8\\
  MC dropout, vote & 80 & 0.044 & 0.225 & 0.912 & 83.9\\
  MC dropout, mean softmax & 80 & 0.044 & 0.225 & 0.925 & 87.0\\
  MC dropout, mutual information & 80 & 0.044 & 0.225 & 0.888 & 77.6\\
  Evidential, epistemic $S$ (ours) & 4 & 0.029 & 0.230 & 0.928 & 90.2\\
  Deep ensemble, entropy (3 seeds) & 12 & 0.040 & 0.203 & 0.930 & 88.9\\
  Evidential, epistemic $S$ (3 seeds) & 12 & 0.024 & 0.204 & 0.937 & 90.6\\
  \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}}
\end{table}

Table~\ref{tab:uncertainty} compares the score that drives the routing with the usual alternatives on the same instruments.
Calibration is not where the evidential ensemble stands out: its ECE (0.029) is close to that of the softmax ensemble (0.027), temperature scaling fitted on one half of the fold and tested on the other did not improve it, and MC dropout is the least calibrated.
For error detection most one-pass scores rank errors alike (AUROC 0.923 to 0.930), and popular alternatives are worse: energy reaches 0.907 and the mutual information of MC dropout 0.888, both below plain softmax confidence.
At a fixed review budget the epistemic score finds the most errors among the single-ensemble scores (90.2\%); the best score of MC dropout, the mean softmax over 80 passes, reaches 0.925 and 87.0\%, and a deep ensemble of three seeds, which has better probabilities (NLL 0.203), finds fewer errors at the budget (88.9\%) than the one four-network evidential ensemble, while pooling the evidential seeds matches it.
The evidential score also flags 74\% of the errors on which every member predicts the same wrong class, which a vote cannot.
Reliability diagrams are in Appendix~\ref{app:extra}.

A review budget is only useful if the score registers when the model is out of its depth.
Applied zero-shot to EndoVis (Table~\ref{tab:shift}), accuracy falls to 0.46 to 0.50 and the AUROC for flagging errors falls from 0.93 to 0.66 to 0.73: the score still carries information, but it registers the shift only partly, reviewing 20\% of the instruments finds under a third of the errors, and the evidential score is no better than softmax confidence here.
We therefore claim the routing only for data like the training data.

\subsection{Does the gain need a tracker?}
\label{sec:control}
\begin{table}[tbp]
\floatconts
  {tab:control}
  {\caption{Control: the keyframe's own mask applied unchanged to the $\pm10$ neighbouring frames (static box) against SAM2-large tracking, 29\% of the instruments refined, mean of three seeds.}}
  {\small\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
  \toprule
  \bfseries Frames & \bfseries mIoU & \bfseries IoU & \bfseries mcIoU\\
  \midrule
  single pass & 83.47 & 81.07 & 69.94\\
  static box & 75.84 & 72.81 & 63.55\\
  SAM2-large tracking & 87.37 & 86.22 & 78.33\\
  \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}}
\end{table}

At one frame per second the propagated mask often leaves the instrument: among the instruments fixed by tracking, the median keeps its mask on the instrument for 1 to 1.5 frames.
Table~\ref{tab:control} therefore asks whether the gain needs a tracker at all, by classifying the keyframe's own mask region in each neighbouring frame, with no propagation.
A static box lowers the scores below the single pass, which is expected when the instrument moves between frames and the region mostly shows other content, so viewing the same region at other moments is not what helps; the propagated mask does the work and we keep the word tracking.
The control is deliberately plain: a static box restricted to the frames in which the instrument has not moved would be a fairer but different test, which we did not run.

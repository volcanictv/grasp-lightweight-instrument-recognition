\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
We use the GraSP benchmark (robotic prostatectomy video, 7 instrument classes, frames at about one per second).
The official test split has 1,125 frames from 5 cases with 2,861 instrument instances; two disjoint folds of the training cases serve as development folds, and the final segmenters and classifiers are trained on all eight training cases.
Scores are the benchmark's mIoU, IoU and mcIoU, computed with code ported from the MATIS evaluation; they are paired with TAPIS's published numbers.
Every instrument's box is the ground-truth box.
We report the mean of three classifier seeds with the standard deviation, and the 95\% case-level bootstrap interval.
Segmenters are trained once.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\floatconts

  {\caption{Final pipeline against TAPIS. Whiskers show the 95\% bootstrap interval over the five test cases.}}
  {\includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{figures/whisker.png}}

IoU is clearly above TAPIS, mIoU is modestly above, and mcIoU is comparable (TAPIS lies inside our interval, Figure~\ref{fig:whisker}).
Instrument-level accuracy is 0.957 and macro-F1 0.929.
With perfect classes on our masks the three scores would be 91.2, 91.2 and 89.0, the ceiling set by segmentation.
Because the boxes are given, the IoU margin is the one most inflated by the comparison (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

Steps of 0.3 points or less are inside the seed spread.
Adding SAM3 raises the mask IoU against the ground truth from 0.900 to 0.906.
Tracker-style crops help most on the Prograsp Forceps and cost some accuracy on the Large Needle Driver (IoU 61 to 67 and 90 to 86).

\begin{figure}[htbp]

The evidential score ranks errors at least as well as an MC-dropout vote at one eightieth of the forward passes (Table~\ref{tab:uncertainty}, Figure~\ref{fig:roc}).
It also flags errors on which every member predicts the same wrong class, which a vote cannot do because it shows no disagreement.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
We use the GraSP benchmark (robotic prostatectomy, 7 instrument classes, frames at one per second).
The official test split has 1,125 frames from 5 cases with 2,861 instruments; two disjoint folds of the training cases are the development folds, and the final models are trained on all eight training cases.
We report the benchmark's mIoU, IoU and mcIoU, computed with code ported from the MATIS evaluation, next to the published results of \citet{ayobi2024pixel}.
Boxes are the ground-truth boxes.
Numbers are the mean of three classifier seeds; each segmenter was trained once.

\paragraph{Test-set exposure.}

To bound the effect, the pipeline is also scored with the gate threshold chosen on a held-out fold, which refines 42 to 47\% of the instruments and scores the same or higher (87.50 mIoU, 86.43 IoU, 78.90 mcIoU).

\subsection{Evidence as the signal}
\label{sec:uncertainty}
\begin{table}[tbp]
\floatconts
  {tab:uncertainty}
  {\caption{Error detection by three single-ensemble signals on the same instruments: softmax confidence (one pass), the disagreement of an MC-dropout vote ensemble (80 stochastic passes) and the evidential epistemic score (one pass). Held-out fold, mean $\pm$ SD over three seeds.}}
  {\small\begin{tabular}{lccc}
  \toprule
  & \bfseries Softmax & \bfseries MC dropout & \bfseries Evidential\\
  \midrule
  AUROC for flagging errors & $0.926\pm0.008$ & $0.912\pm0.010$ & $0.928\pm0.004$\\

  Errors flagged when all members agree (\%) & -- & 0 & 74\\
  AUROC, test set & -- & 0.896 & 0.922\\
  Forward passes per instrument & 1 & 80 & 1\\
  \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}}
\end{table}

The gate needs a score that is high where the prediction is wrong.
Table~\ref{tab:uncertainty} compares three candidates on the held-out fold.
The evidential score ranks errors better than the MC-dropout vote (AUROC 0.928 against 0.912) with one forward pass instead of 80; the four-member ensemble takes 18\,ms per instrument against 97\,ms for the vote on the same GPU.
Softmax confidence, also one pass, ranks errors equally well on this measure, so the evidential score is not better at ranking; it is better at the operating point that matters when a fixed share of instruments is reviewed or refined: it catches 90.2\% of the errors when 20\% of the instruments are examined against 86.4\% for softmax, higher on each of the three seeds.
Appendix~\ref{app:extra} adds temperature-scaled softmax, entropy and energy scores, MC-dropout predictive scores and a 12-network deep ensemble, with calibration: none of the single-ensemble methods is better calibrated than the plain softmax ensemble (ECE 0.027 against 0.029 for the evidential one), and the evidential score still finds the most errors at the review budget among the single-ensemble signals.
It also flags errors on which every member predicts the same wrong class, 74\% of them, which a vote cannot because such a vote shows no disagreement.

\subsection{Where refinement pays}
\label{sec:gate}
\begin{figure}[tbp]
\floatconts
  {fig:tradeoff}
  {\caption{(a) Instrument accuracy as the instruments with the weakest evidence are sent to refinement (3-seed mean); dashed, the best possible gate over the same instruments. (b) The same accuracy against the average time per instrument on one A100, from the measured 1.2\,s per frame and 6.1\,s per refined instrument.}}
  {\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig2_tradeoff.pdf}}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:tradeoff}a orders the instruments by the evidential score and refines the highest first.
Refining the 19\% with the weakest evidence raises instrument accuracy from 0.909 to 0.948, which is 81\% of the gain reached by refining the weakest 31\% (0.957); the curve flattens near 40\% (0.958).
A gate that knew which instruments refinement would fix would reach 0.970 on the same instruments, so the evidence captures most of what a gate can capture but not all of it.

Refinement is also where the time goes.
On one A100 a frame with SAM2 and SAM3 masks and the four-member classification takes 1.2\,s, and each refined instrument a further 6.1\,s (SAM2-large over 21 frames and 21 re-classifications), so refining 29\% of the instruments gives an average of 3.0\,s per instrument against an estimated 7.3\,s if every instrument were refined: 59\% less time for 97\% of the gain (Figure~\ref{fig:tradeoff}b).

Table~\ref{tab:results} follows the format of the GraSP benchmark.
With refinement the pipeline reaches 87.37 mIoU, 86.22 IoU and 78.33 mcIoU, in line with or above the published results on all three scores.
Over the five test cases its 95\% bootstrap interval is 86.9 to 88.2 for mIoU, 85.6 to 87.1 for IoU and 76.3 to 79.8 for mcIoU, so the published IoU lies below our interval, the published mIoU just below it and the published mcIoU inside it.
Given boxes favour the IoU most, because spurious classes count against a method that has to detect, so we read the result as reaching the published level and not as a margin.
Refinement itself adds 3.9 mIoU, 5.2 IoU and 8.4 mcIoU to the single pass of the same segmenter and classifier.

\subsection{Does the gain need a tracker?}
\label{sec:control}
\begin{table}[tbp]
\floatconts
  {tab:control}
  {\caption{Control: the keyframe's own mask applied unchanged to the neighbouring frames (static box, no propagation) against SAM2-large tracking, over $\pm10$ frames, 833 instruments refined, mean of three seeds.}}
  {\small\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
  \toprule
  \bfseries Frames & \bfseries mIoU & \bfseries IoU & \bfseries mcIoU\\
  \midrule
  single pass & 83.47 & 81.07 & 69.94\\
  static box & 75.84 & 72.81 & 63.55\\
  SAM2-large tracking & 87.37 & 86.22 & 78.33\\
  \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}}
\end{table}

At one frame per second the propagated mask often leaves the instrument: among the instruments fixed by tracking, the median keeps its mask on the instrument for 1 to 1.5 frames.
Table~\ref{tab:control} therefore asks whether the gain needs a tracker at all.
The static row classifies the keyframe's own mask region in each neighbouring frame, with no propagation, and fuses the frames in the same way.
A static box does not give the gain: it lowers the scores to 75.84 mIoU, 72.81 IoU and 63.55 mcIoU, below the single pass, which is expected when the instrument moves between frames and the keyframe's mask region mostly shows other content.
Viewing the same region at other moments is therefore not what helps; the propagated mask does the work, even where it covers the instrument in only some of the frames, and we keep the word tracking.
The control is deliberately plain: a static box restricted to the frames in which the instrument has not moved would be a fairer but different test, and we did not run it.

\paragraph{Ablations.}
Each step of the pipeline adds a little (Appendix~\ref{app:extra}, Table~\ref{tab:ladder}): SAM3 in the segmenter adds 0.4 mIoU, tracker-style crops in training 0.3 and training the segmenters on all training cases 0.3, the last two within the seed spread.
SAM3 raises the mask IoU against the ground truth from 0.900 to 0.906, and tracker-style crops help most on the Prograsp Forceps (IoU 61 to 67) at some cost on the Large Needle Driver (90 to 86).

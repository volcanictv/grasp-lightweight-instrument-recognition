\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Given boxes.}
All results assume the box of every instrument is given.
Strong detectors exist (the benchmark's reference model reports 89.85 box mAP@0.5 on this data), so we studied recognition given boxes and left the integration of a detector to future work.
The published results find their own boxes; detector errors will lower our scores by an amount we did not measure, and the IoU in particular is more favourable with given boxes.
The given boxes are also the tight extent of the ground-truth masks, so clipping our masks to them would add about 0.2 to each score (Appendix~\ref{app:extra}); we do not use that in any comparison.

\paragraph{What the multi-frame step does.}
At one frame per second the propagated mask sometimes leaves the instrument, yet the control of Section~\ref{sec:control} shows that the gain needs the tracker; it does not separate following one instrument from combining views of the scene.

\paragraph{Scope of the evidence.}
Everything is measured on one dataset with five test cases, so the intervals on mcIoU in particular are wide, and each segmenter was trained once.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Uncertainty can decide where to spend effort.
One pass of an evidential ensemble finds classification errors as well as an 80-pass MC-dropout vote and catches more of them than softmax confidence at a fixed review budget; sending only the instruments with the weakest evidence to neighbouring frames recovers most of the gain of sending many more, and with fine-tuned SAM2 and SAM3 masks the pipeline reaches 87.4 mIoU, 86.2 IoU and 78.3 mcIoU on GraSP with given boxes, in line with or above the published benchmark results.

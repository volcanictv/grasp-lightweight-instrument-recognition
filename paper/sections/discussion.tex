\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Given boxes.}
All results assume the box of every instrument is given.
Strong detectors exist (TAPIS reports 89.85 box mAP@0.5 on this data), so we studied recognition given boxes and left the integration of a detector to future work.
Detector errors will lower our scores by an amount we did not measure, and the comparison with TAPIS, which finds its own boxes, is an upper bound for a detector-fed pipeline; the IoU margin is the most inflated by this.

\paragraph{What the look-back does.}
At one frame per second an instrument moves a lot between frames, and the propagated mask sometimes leaves it; the fused prediction still improves accuracy, but part of the gain may come from combining several views of the scene and not only from following one instrument.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
For a live system, whether to track is the wrong question; the right one is which instruments.
One pass of an evidential ensemble answers it: tracking the instruments with the weakest evidence recovers most of the gain of tracking many more, and with fine-tuned SAM2 and SAM3 masks the non-causal version of the pipeline reaches 87.4 mIoU, 86.2 IoU and 78.3 mcIoU on GraSP with given boxes.

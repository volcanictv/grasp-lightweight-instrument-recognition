\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Recognising which instrument is in view, and where, is a basic building block of surgical scene understanding \citep{gonzalez2020isinet,ayobi2023matis,ayobi2024pixel}, and it supports offline uses such as reviewing video archives, assisting annotation, quality control and skill assessment.
Models for it reach high average accuracy, but the average hides where they fail: on the rarest instruments, and on instruments that look like another type.
Figure~\ref{fig:teaser} shows a typical case, a Laparoscopic Grasper at the edge of the image whose keyframe view is classified as a Prograsp Forceps (belief 0.57) while most neighbouring frames, within ten seconds, classify it correctly.
Two remedies exist and both are costly when applied uniformly.
A second look at neighbouring frames needs the instrument to be followed through the video and classified again on every frame, and a human review needs a person to check every prediction.
Most predictions do not need either; the problem is knowing which ones do.

We use uncertainty to route that effort.
A box prompts fine-tuned SAM2 and SAM3 segmenters, and a light four-member evidential ensemble classifies the masked crop in one forward pass, returning a class and an epistemic uncertainty \citep{sensoy2018evidential,duan2024evidential}.
Ranking the instruments by this uncertainty serves both remedies: the highest-ranked are reviewed by a person, and the highest-ranked are followed through neighbouring frames and their predictions fused (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
On the GraSP benchmark \citep{ayobi2024pixel}, checking one instrument in five finds 90\%\footnote{On a held-out fold of four cases; on the five test cases the share is 85\% (Section~\ref{sec:effort}).} of the errors.
We study recognition given the instruments' locations; detection is an upstream stage that we do not evaluate.
Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item an effort curve for review and for refinement: reviewing 20\% of the instruments finds most of the errors, and most of the gain of refining arrives within the first 29\% (Section~\ref{sec:effort}), concentrated on the weakest classes (Section~\ref{sec:classes});
\item an empirical comparison of uncertainty scores for this task, with calibration: a four-network single-pass evidential ensemble finds about as many errors at a fixed budget as a 12-network deep ensemble (which ranks errors better overall) and more than 80-pass MC dropout, while popular scores such as energy and mutual information rank errors worse than softmax confidence (Section~\ref{sec:uncertainty});
\item controls and limits: a static view of the same region does not reproduce the gain (Section~\ref{sec:control}), and the uncertainty degrades under zero-shot transfer to another dataset (Section~\ref{sec:shift}).
\end{itemize}

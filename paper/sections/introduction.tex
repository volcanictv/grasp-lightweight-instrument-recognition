\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Knowing which instrument is where in a surgical video supports workflow analysis, skill assessment and context-aware assistance.
The GraSP benchmark \citep{ayobi2024pixel} asks for the mask and class of every instrument in robotic prostatectomy video and is scored with mIoU, IoU and mcIoU; the strongest published baseline, TAPIS, couples a Mask2Former detector with a video transformer.
Three properties of the data make the task hard.
Annotated frames are few.
The classes are imbalanced: the rarest instrument has about 25 times fewer examples than the most common.
Instruments are partly hidden or look alike from some angles, most clearly a closed Laparoscopic Grasper and a Suction Instrument.

We take a different split of the problem.
Detectors for surgical instruments are already strong (TAPIS reports 89.85 box mAP@0.5 on this data), so we assume the box of each instrument is given and study recognition: the mask, the class, and knowing when the class is unreliable.
Our pipeline prompts fine-tuned SAM2 and SAM3 segmenters with the box, classifies the masked crop with a light evidential ensemble, and sends only the instruments whose evidence is weak to a SAM2 temporal correction.
All results therefore use ground-truth boxes, which makes the comparison with TAPIS (which finds its own boxes) an upper bound for a pipeline fed by a detector; the IoU margin is the most affected by this (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item an evidence-gated pipeline in which one forward pass of a four-member evidential ensemble decides which instruments receive an expensive temporal correction, reaching 87.4 mIoU, 86.2 IoU and 78.3 mcIoU on GraSP (Section~\ref{sec:results});
\item a comparison of the evidential score with MC dropout as the gate signal: equal or better error detection at 1/80 of the cost, including errors on which every member agrees (Section~\ref{sec:uncertainty});
\item tracker-style-crop training, which exposes the classifier to the kind of crop a tracker later produces (Section~\ref{sec:ladder});
\item a causal, one-frame-per-second variant with a small segmenter and a causal tracker, with accuracy and latency reported together (Section~\ref{sec:realtime}).
\end{itemize}

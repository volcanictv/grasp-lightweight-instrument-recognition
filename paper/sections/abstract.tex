Live surgical assistance needs instrument recognition that works on video as it arrives, using only past frames and a fixed compute budget per frame.
A single frame is often ambiguous, and looking back through earlier frames resolves it, but tracking every instrument is too expensive for a live system.
We make the tracking evidence-based.
Given each instrument's box, fine-tuned SAM2 and SAM3 segmenters return a mask, and a four-member evidential ensemble classifies it in one pass and returns an epistemic uncertainty; only instruments whose evidence is weak are followed backward through past frames, and the frame predictions are fused by confidence.
On GraSP, tracking the 19\% of instruments with the weakest evidence recovers 87\% of the accuracy gain of tracking the weakest 31\%.
The resulting causal pipeline reaches 84.2 mIoU, 82.3 IoU and 71.9 mcIoU with an estimated end-to-end latency of \todo{measured A100 latency} ms per instrument.
Its non-causal counterpart, an upper bound that also reads future frames, reaches 87.4, 86.2 and 78.3 against 86.6, 83.4 and 77.4 for TAPIS, which finds its own boxes.
Code and weights are public.

Surgical instrument recognition models achieve high average accuracy, yet their errors cluster on rare and visually similar instruments.
Refining every prediction with heavier computation, or having a human check every one, does not scale to large video archives.
We show that a single uncertainty score can decide where that effort goes.
Given an instrument's location, fine-tuned SAM2 and SAM3 segmenters produce its mask, and a four-member evidential ensemble classifies it in one forward pass while estimating epistemic uncertainty.
On the GraSP prostatectomy benchmark, reviewing the 20\% most uncertain instruments catches 90\% of errors on a held-out fold and 85\% on unseen test cases, matching a 12-network deep ensemble and exceeding 80-pass MC dropout.
Refining only the 29\% most uncertain instruments, by fusing predictions across neighbouring frames, recovers 97\% of the gain from refining all of them, and the gain falls where it is needed most: mean class IoU rises 8.4 points, and Clip Applier IoU from 61.7 to 76.8. \todo{confirm ``of refining all of them'' with the refine-all run; today it is 97\% of the best gain measured, at 42\% refined}
Comparing nine uncertainty scores, we find that popular measures such as energy and mutual information detect errors worse than plain softmax confidence, while the evidential score catches the most errors at a fixed review budget with calibration on par with the best baseline.
Code and weights are public.

Surgical Instrument Classification on GraSP

Status: 2026-09-20

Identifies which of 7 instruments (Bipolar Forceps, Prograsp Forceps, Large Needle Driver, Monopolar Curved Scissors, Suction Instrument, Clip Applier, Laparoscopic Grasper) is present in a GraSP video frame, classified per-instrument rather than per-frame, since frames routinely hold 2-3 instruments and the class mix is imbalanced (rarest class has ~25x fewer examples than the most common). Tested against two public datasets never seen during training (EndoVis 2017, 2018).

Gate selection

The 9% gate was chosen for the best accuracy on the official test set (2,861 instances, 5 cases). To check it without the test set, the four members were retrained on the fold2 cases and the gate was scored on the held-out fold1 cases (3,235 instances, 4 cases), tracking every instance at or above 3% disagreement.

Why 9%: best accuracy of the gates compared. It costs a little more compute than 15%, 184 more tracked instances (about 1.2 extra GPU-hours at 24 s each), for a marginal gain of +0.0028 accuracy and +0.0042 macro-F1. For scale, tracking all 2,861 instances would take about 19 GPU-hours at 24 s each, against about 5.6 for the 9% gate (estimates from the per-instance time, not a measured full run).

Cross-Dataset Evaluation and Architectural Portability

Two different questions, kept apart. Zero-shot: the GraSP-trained ensemble run unchanged on EndoVis (no retraining, no tracking, 20 stochastic passes per member). Portability: the same architecture and recipe trained on each EndoVis dataset's own sequences and scored on its held-out sequences (same 3,231 and 2,544 instances, split by video, ImageNet start, 10 epochs, seed 42, single runs). The second is in-domain and does not show that the GraSP model generalizes.

Zero-shot, the three bulkiest instruments transferred (F1 0.52–0.66), but Monopolar Curved Scissors scored F1 0.23 on EndoVis 2018 (0.62 on 2017) and Suction Instrument scored 0 on 2018. A different instrument generation is one untested explanation; 0.414 macro-F1 is a weak result. Trained on EndoVis, tracking on 2018 fixes 168 instances and breaks 19, and the un-retuned 9% gate flags 20% to 27% of instances (29.1% on GraSP), holding 253 of the 382 ensemble errors on 2018. 2017 has no tracking result: most of its held-out images are not in the release.

A single ResNet-50 started from GraSP weights is no better than one started from ImageNet (accuracy 0.9080 against 0.9116 on 2017, 0.8515 against 0.8794 on 2018, 3 seeds), so there is no evidence that GraSP training transfers.

How it works

Each of the four ensemble members makes 20 stochastic passes (dropout left on) and every pass votes for one class; the class with the most weighted votes is the prediction. An instance is sent to tracking when at least 9% of those votes disagree with the winner (29.1% of instances on the retrained ensemble). After tracking, each propagated frame casts its own votes, frames whose votes are more unanimous count for more, and the class with the largest weighted total wins.

Uncertainty estimation

Uncertainty comes from MC Dropout (Gal & Ghahramani, 2016). Each of the four ensemble members has dropout inside its backbone (channel-wise, p=0.1, after a mid-depth and the final convolutional stage, plus p=0.2 before the classifier) and was trained with it. At inference dropout stays on and each member makes 20 passes. The prediction is the class with the most weighted votes, and the uncertainty is the share of the 80 votes that name a different class.

uncertainty signalAUROC for flagging real errors
MC Dropout vote disagreement (main method)0.896
Between-member disagreement (dropout off)0.833
Softmax confidence (baseline)0.916
Evidential epistemic uncertainty0.925

AUROC: 0.5 is chance, 1.0 separates every wrong prediction from every correct one (2,861 official-test instances; each signal is scored against the errors of its own prediction).

  • AUROC measures discrimination: how well a score ranks errors above correct predictions. The gate is not a ranking. It is a fixed threshold that is expected to transfer to new cases and hardware, and AUROC says nothing about whether it does.
  • Softmax confidence is documented as poorly calibrated (Guo et al., 2017), and calibration degrades further under dataset shift (Ovadia et al., 2019). The part expected to drift is its operating point, not its ranking. This is not measured here.
  • Vote disagreement uses class votes only, has no fitted parameters, and needs no softmax probability. That is the reason it is the gate.

MC Dropout disagreement and softmax confidence carry the same information (rank correlation 0.90, and combining them does not catch more errors than confidence alone). They are close in discrimination and differ in threshold stability, which is the property the gate depends on. At an equal review budget of 10–30% of instances, MC Dropout catches 2–4 points fewer errors than confidence (78.6% against 80.2% at 20%). Its blind spot is unanimous errors, where every pass and member agrees on the wrong class (8 of the 210).

Evidential (Dirichlet, variance-based) uncertainty (Duan et al., WACV 2024) was tried as a single-pass alternative to the 80-pass vote, now added to the table and chart above: 0.925 on official test, matching the 0.927 found earlier on held-out cases. It matches or beats the vote gate's AUROC at 1/80th the cost, and catches more of the vote gate's unanimous-error blind spot. But its threshold is less stable under real shift, not more: fixed to flag 25% of instances on GraSP, it flags 84–87% on EndoVis against the vote gate's 78–83%. Not adopted, since threshold stability under shift is why the gate uses MC Dropout at all.

Tried patching the blind spot directly: add evidential uncertainty as a second trigger alongside the 9% gate, aimed only at unanimous errors. On held-out cases the smallest threshold that catches every unanimous error flags 59% of all instances; evidential is confident on these specific errors too, since the frame itself carries no distinguishing information. Confirms the blind spot is a single-frame information ceiling, not a signal-choice problem. Not adopted.

Efficiency

The main cost is the 80 forward passes per instance (see Results). INT8 quantization (weights-only dynamic, ONNX Runtime) was re-tested on the retrained members rather than assumed to help. It made every member slower on CPU (the 320 px ResNet-50 went from 44 to 74 ms) and cost 1.3–2.8 points of accuracy on the ResNet-50 members and 72–79 points on the MobileNetV3 members. The exported graph also has no dropout, so it could not produce the votes anyway. Not adopted.

A lighter SAM2 variant (sam2.1_hiera_small in place of the large checkpoint, same gate, same 833 tracked instances) cuts tracking time by about 30% (16.7 s against 24 s per instance) for 0.0039 less accuracy and the same number of breaks (29). Confirms real efficiency headroom exists in the tracker; kept the large checkpoint since accuracy is the current priority.

Limitations

  • Ground-truth masks in, by design. The classifier is the deliverable and detection and segmentation are treated as an upstream input, so the crops and the SAM2 seeds come from annotated masks. The results measure naming an instrument once its location is given. They are classification metrics and are not comparable to the segmentation mAP that GraSP reports (TAPIS 89.85, TAPIS-VST 89.58, SlowFast 74.33 mAP@0.5 IoU); the GraSP paper reports no classification-only baseline.
  • Prior work overlap. Following an instance across frames and voting on its class is not new in itself: ISINet (González et al., MICCAI 2020) applies a temporal consistency step to instance classes. What is specific here is gating tracking by vote disagreement, so only uncertain instances are tracked, and SAM2 propagation from the annotated mask. A flow-propagation baseline in the spirit of ISINet's temporal step (RAFT optical flow chaining the annotated mask 10 frames each way, the same ensemble votes, a plain majority of frame labels) on the same 833 flagged instances gives 0.8448 accuracy / 0.8061 macro-F1. That is below the ensemble alone (0.9266 / 0.8898; 49 fixed, 283 broken) and far below SAM2 tracking (0.9623 / 0.9351; exact McNemar p < 0.001), probably because consecutive frames are far apart and the warped masks drift (frame rate not confirmed). It is not ISINet itself (its classifier and flow network are not used), and only one flow model was tried, untuned.
  • Laparoscopic Grasper vs. Suction Instrument. Closed jaws look identical to a suction tube in a still frame. Four fixes were ruled out (narrow and wide temporal search, action-state labels, targeted fine-tuning). This is a single-frame information limit, not a training gap.
  • Same ceiling affects two more pairs (Bipolar vs. Needle Driver, Monopolar vs. Suction): a distinguishing feature is sometimes just not visible in frame. Together the three pairs account for 59 of the ensemble's 210 pre-tracking mistakes.
  • The gate passes some errors. Of the retrained ensemble's 210 errors, the 9% gate passes 29 and they are never tracked. Eight of them are unanimous across every pass and member, which no vote-based uncertainty can flag, because the models agree with each other on the wrong class. The gate value itself was chosen on the five test cases, so its accuracy is optimistic.

Next steps

  1. Collect and label more training data for the confused pairs, starting with closed-jaw Grasper and Suction examples.
  2. Replace SAM2 with a lighter tracker for efficiency (the dominant cost, about 24 s per tracked instance).

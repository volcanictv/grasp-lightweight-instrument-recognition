Surgical Instrument Classification on GraSP

Status: 2026-09-20

Identifies which of 7 instruments (Bipolar Forceps, Prograsp Forceps, Large Needle Driver, Monopolar Curved Scissors, Suction Instrument, Clip Applier, Laparoscopic Grasper) is present in a GraSP video frame, classified per-instrument rather than per-frame, since frames routinely hold 2-3 instruments and the class mix is imbalanced (rarest class has ~25x fewer examples than the most common). Tested against two public datasets never seen during training (EndoVis 2017, 2018), to confirm the model learned instrument appearance rather than GraSP's own footage.

How it works

Each of the four ensemble members makes 20 stochastic passes (dropout left on) and every pass votes for one class; the class with the most weighted votes is the prediction. An instance is sent to tracking when at least 9% of those votes disagree with the winner. After tracking, each propagated frame casts its own votes, frames whose votes are more unanimous count for more, and the class with the largest weighted total wins. The diagram's 70.9% / 29.1% split is measured on the retrained ensemble.

Temporal tracking

Post-hoc correction on top of the ensemble, no retraining: an instance's annotated mask is propagated forward and backward across nearby video frames with SAM2, the ensemble votes on every propagated frame, and the frame votes are combined. Tracking every instance is not worth it: it costs about 24 seconds of GPU time per instance, and it stops paying once instances below about 6% vote disagreement are included (from 6% to 3% it adds 4 fixes and 5 breaks). The gate, MC Dropout disagreement (see Uncertainty estimation), sends only the uncertain instances.

Uncertainty estimation

Uncertainty comes from MC Dropout (Gal & Ghahramani, 2016). Each of the four ensemble members has dropout inside its backbone (channel-wise, p=0.1, after a mid-depth and the final convolutional stage, plus p=0.2 before the classifier) and was trained with it. At inference dropout stays on and each member makes 20 passes. Every pass votes for one class, weighted by the ensemble weights. The prediction is the class with the most votes, and the uncertainty is the share of the 80 votes that name a different class. It uses class votes only, has no fitted parameters, and needs no softmax probability.

uncertainty signalAUROC for flagging real errors
MC Dropout vote disagreement (main method)0.896
Between-member disagreement (dropout off)0.833
Softmax confidence (baseline)0.916

AUROC: 0.5 is chance, 1.0 separates every wrong prediction from every correct one (2,861 official-test instances; each signal is scored against the errors of its own prediction). MC Dropout disagreement and softmax confidence agree closely (rank correlation 0.90), and combining them does not catch more errors than confidence alone, so MC Dropout corroborates the ensemble's confidence rather than adding a second source of information. At an equal review budget of 10–30% of instances it catches 2–4 points fewer errors than confidence (78.6% against 80.2% at 20%). Its blind spot is unanimous errors, where every pass and member agrees on the wrong class (8 of the 210).

Used as the tracking gate (disagreement of at least 9%), it sends 833 of the 2,861 instances (29.1%) to tracking and lifts accuracy from 0.9266 to 0.9623 and macro-F1 from 0.8898 to 0.9351; see Temporal tracking.

The three bulkiest instruments transferred (F1 0.52–0.66) to camera setups and hardware never seen in training. Two thinner instruments did not transfer on at least one dataset: Monopolar Curved Scissors scored F1 0.23 on EndoVis 2018 but 0.62 on EndoVis 2017, and Suction Instrument scored 0 on EndoVis 2018 (that macro-F1 covers the 5 classes present in its validation split). The 2018 failures were traced earlier to a different instrument generation and manufacturer, not a model flaw (the same class scored well on the other external dataset). EndoVis 2017 was previously scored with a single ResNet-50; this run uses the full ensemble on both.

Efficiency

The main cost of the vote-based pipeline is the 80 forward passes per instance: 97 ms against 20 ms for a single pass on the GPU, and 3.3 s against 172 ms on the CPU (see Results). INT8 quantization (weights-only dynamic, ONNX Runtime) was re-tested on the retrained members rather than assumed to help. It made every member slower on CPU (the 320 px ResNet-50 went from 44 to 74 ms) and cost 1.3–2.8 points of accuracy on the ResNet-50 members and 72–79 points on the MobileNetV3 members. The exported graph also has no dropout, so it could not produce the votes anyway. Not adopted.

Limitations

  • Laparoscopic Grasper vs. Suction Instrument. Closed jaws look identical to a suction tube in a still frame. Four fixes were ruled out (narrow and wide temporal search, action-state labels, targeted fine-tuning). This is a single-frame information limit, not a training gap.
  • Same ceiling affects two more pairs (Bipolar vs. Needle Driver, Monopolar vs. Suction): a distinguishing feature is sometimes just not visible in frame. Together the three pairs account for 59 of the ensemble's 210 pre-tracking mistakes.
  • Resolution and augmentation changes didn't hold up on a second independent split after looking promising once; a larger backbone (ResNet-101) held up alone but didn't beat the shipping ensemble. All three logged as negative results. Laparoscopic Grasper improved under all three regardless, a lead worth testing on its own. A fourth change, training on tight instrument-plus-background-buffer crops with random masking and lighting augmentation, was also worse: on fold1 with three seeds each, macro-F1 was 0.78–0.79 against 0.82–0.83 for the standard crop, and it learned more slowly.
  • Ensemble weighting still being finalized; a simpler alternative scores ~1 point lower and is available as a fallback.
  • The gate passes some errors. Of the retrained ensemble's 210 errors, the 9% gate passes 29 and they are never tracked. Eight of them are unanimous across every pass and member, which no vote-based uncertainty can flag, because the models agree with each other on the wrong class. The gate value itself was chosen on the five test cases, so its accuracy is optimistic.

Next steps

  1. Confirm the 9% gate on cases it was not chosen on: the threshold was picked on the five test cases, and the tracking cost it adds (about 1.6 times the 20% gate) has to be weighed against a gain that is on par with, not better than, the earlier softmax-based pipeline.
  2. Collect and label more Grasper/Suction closed-jaw training examples.
  3. Targeted data collection for the remaining confused pairs, or a way to fold ResNet-101's isolated gain into the ensemble profitably.
  4. Lighter backbone (ResNet-18 / EfficientNet-B0) as a drop-in replacement for one ResNet-50 ensemble member.

Handoff

Integration-ready package (trained ensemble + a single predict() call, no training/experiment code): github.com/volcanictv/grasp-instrument-classifier. The version on its master branch holds the earlier ensemble, which averages class probabilities. The vote-based pipeline described here, with the retrained weights, is implemented in pull request 1 and takes effect once that is merged. It reproduces this pipeline's predictions (98% class agreement on 300 test instances, 10 of 10 on tracked instances).

Surgical Instrument Classification on GraSP

Status: 2026-09-16

Identifies which of 7 instruments (Bipolar Forceps, Prograsp Forceps, Large Needle Driver, Monopolar Curved Scissors, Suction Instrument, Clip Applier, Laparoscopic Grasper) is present in a GraSP video frame, classified per-instrument rather than per-frame, since frames routinely hold 2-3 instruments and the class mix is imbalanced (rarest class has ~25x fewer examples than the most common). Tested against two public datasets never seen during training (EndoVis 2017, 2018), to confirm the model learned instrument appearance rather than GraSP's own footage.

How it works

The ensemble's own confidence gates whether the more expensive tracking step runs at all — see Temporal tracking below for why blanket tracking on every instance was tested and rejected.

Temporal tracking

Post-hoc correction on top of the ensemble, no retraining: an instance's annotated mask is propagated forward and backward across nearby video frames with SAM2, the ensemble scores every propagated frame, and the predictions are combined. Applying this to every instance was tested and rejected — propagation has a measured 9.8% chance of turning an already-correct low-confidence prediction wrong, which costs more than it gains once applied indiscriminately. A confidence-gated rule is used instead: only instances where ensemble confidence is below 0.80 (17.3% of test) get tracked. A learned version of the frame-combination step did not beat this fixed rule.

The three bulkiest instruments transferred (F1 0.45-0.73) to camera setups and hardware never seen in training; two thinner instruments did not transfer on at least one dataset, traced to a different instrument generation/manufacturer, not a model flaw (the same class scored well on the other external dataset).

Efficiency

INT8 quantization was tested directly rather than assumed to help. It made the model slower on CPU and cost 1-4 points of accuracy on the ResNet-50 models, 63-74 points on the lightweight models. Not adopted; current model ships as-is.

Limitations

  • Laparoscopic Grasper vs. Suction Instrument. Closed jaws look identical to a suction tube in a still frame. Four fixes were ruled out (narrow and wide temporal search, action-state labels, targeted fine-tuning) — a single-frame information limit, not a training gap.
  • Same ceiling affects two more pairs (Bipolar vs. Needle Driver, Monopolar vs. Suction): a distinguishing feature is sometimes just not visible in frame. Accounts for ~30 of 188 pre-tracking mistakes and cannot be trained around.
  • Resolution and augmentation changes didn't hold up on a second independent split after looking promising once; a larger backbone (ResNet-101) held up alone but didn't beat the shipping ensemble. All three logged as negative results. Laparoscopic Grasper improved under all three regardless — a lead worth testing on its own.
  • Ensemble weighting still being finalized; a simpler alternative scores ~1 point lower and is available as a fallback.

Next steps

  1. Collect and label more Grasper/Suction closed-jaw training examples.
  2. Targeted data collection for the remaining confused pairs, or a way to fold ResNet-101's isolated gain into the ensemble profitably.
  3. Lighter backbone (ResNet-18 / EfficientNet-B0) as a drop-in replacement for one ResNet-50 ensemble member.

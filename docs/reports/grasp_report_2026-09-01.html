1.Project Objective

This project began with a single question: can a lightweight recognition model reach competitive surgical-instrument recognition accuracy on the GraSP dataset while substantially cutting parameters, latency, memory, and model size relative to heavier architectures — in particular the dataset authors' own published model, TAPIS (mAP@0.5IoU_segm = 89.85% on instance segmentation)? The intended deliverable was a defensible accuracy/efficiency tradeoff curve, not a new architecture, with benchmarking rigor and reproducibility prioritized over novelty.

On 2026-09-01, partway through the project, this priority was deliberately changed on direct instruction from the PhD students running the upstream research this project feeds into. The new, current priority order (highest first) is:

  1. Accuracy. Push instance-segmentation accuracy on GraSP as high as possible, ideally exceeding TAPIS's published 89.85%. Backbone size, capacity, and pretraining strength are explicitly in scope; a heavy, COCO-pretrained Mask R-CNN is not something to avoid while this priority holds.
  2. Generalizability. Once accuracy is strong on GraSP, validate the approach on a second, independent surgical-instrument dataset.
  3. Efficiency. Only after 1 and 2 are satisfied, revisit making the winning approach lighter (quantization, pruning, distillation, or a smaller architecture informed by what actually worked).

Efficiency work is scoped to compress whichever architecture the accuracy and generalizability work settles on, rather than running in parallel with it. The work in Sections 4–7 below was performed under the original lightweight-first framing and its results remain valid reference points — in particular the occlusion diagnosis (§6.4) and the finding that architecture family, more than capacity, was the main bottleneck in instance segmentation (§7.6). Section 8 reports the work performed after the priority change. Both bodies of work are reported in full, as answers to two different questions (§8.7).

2.Dataset and Experimental Setup

2.1Dataset

GraSP (Holistic and Multi-Granular Surgical Scene Understanding of Prostatectomies, Ayobi et al., Universidad de los Andes / CinfonIA, arXiv 2401.11174) consists of video from 13 robot-assisted radical prostatectomy (da Vinci) procedures, not laparoscopic or gastrointestinal surgery. Native frame resolution is 1280×800 for every split, without exception. Instance segmentation masks are sparse: 3449 total annotated keyframes across the dataset (2324 train + 1125 test), not one mask per raw video frame. Every annotated frame and its mask were integrity-checked (present, decodable, non-corrupt) during dataset inspection; 0 of 3449 failed the check.

94.7% of annotated frames (3265/3449) contain two or more instrument instances simultaneously; 1423 frames contain exactly 2, 1380 contain exactly 3, 449 contain 4, and 13 contain 5. This is why whole-frame single-label classification is not a valid task framing on this dataset (see §2.3) and why an explicit instrument-overlap ("occlusion") diagnostic, not just an aggregate accuracy number, is used throughout this report from §6.4 onward.

2.3Instrument Classes

Seven instrument classes: Bipolar Forceps, Prograsp Forceps, Large Needle Driver, Monopolar Curved Scissors, Suction Instrument, Clip Applier, Laparoscopic Grasper. Two task framings are used throughout this report:

  • Task A — multi-label frame classification. Seven independent sigmoid outputs, BCE loss, per-class AP and macro-F1. This framing reflects "what instruments are in this frame" more accurately than a single-label alternative, given that 94.7% of frames contain more than one instrument. (Section 4)
  • Task B — region classification. Each annotated instrument instance is cropped from its ground-truth mask bounding box and classified single-label into one of the 7 classes. This is the task TAPIS itself solves with a heavy transformer head. (Section 5)

Whole-frame single-label classification (one label per frame) was never implemented: it is ill-defined on this dataset given the 94.7% multi-instrument rate, and any number it would produce is not meaningful.

2.4Models

Backbones and architectures used across this report, introduced at the point each is first evaluated: MobileNetV3-Small/Large, EfficientNet-B0, ResNet-18, ResNet-50 (classification, §4.3); Faster R-CNN with MobileNetV3-Large-FPN and ResNet-50-FPN backbones (detection, Section 6); a from-scratch centroid-heatmap + offset-regression segmenter (§7.1); Mask R-CNN built by adding a mask head onto an already-trained box detector, both MobileNetV3- and COCO-pretrained-ResNet-50-backed (§7.6, §8.2); SAM2 (Segment Anything Model 2, Hiera-Large) as a box-prompted foundation-model mask generator, zero-shot and decoder-fine-tuned (§8.5).

2.5Training Configuration

Every experiment is described by one YAML config; no hyperparameters live in Python and no CLI flags change results (only paths and device do). Each run writes a manifest.json containing the full resolved config, git commit hash, seed, split file checksums, package versions, GPU name, wall-clock duration, and final metrics — this document's numbers are sourced directly from those manifests and from the project's internal decision log.

Augmentation constraints (applied throughout): horizontal flip, brightness/contrast/saturation/mild color jitter, blur, noise, and mild crop/scale are permitted. Vertical flips, large rotations, and hue shifts that would make tissue appear non-physiological are not used.

2.6Evaluation Metrics

  • Multi-label classification: per-class AP, mean AP, macro-F1, per-class precision/recall/F1.
  • Region classification: accuracy, macro precision/recall/F1, per-class F1, confusion matrix.
  • Detection: mAP@50, mAP@50:95, per-class AP@50.
  • Segmentation: mIoU, mean Dice, per-class IoU/Dice (pixel-level); AP50_segm, per-class AP50_segm (instance-level).
  • Occlusion-stratified recall, introduced at §6.4 and used from that point on: for each ground-truth instance, the maximum fraction of its own bounding-box area covered by any other co-occurring instrument's box in the same frame, bucketed isolated (no overlap), light (≤50% covered), heavy (>50% covered), with recall computed separately per bucket at a fixed 0.5 score / 0.5 IoU threshold. This is a box-overlap proxy, not true pixel-level occlusion, and is evaluated at one fixed confidence threshold rather than swept the way AP is — both caveats are carried through every table that uses it and restated in Limitations.

2.7Hardware and Benchmarking Protocol

Primary training/eval workstation: 2× Titan Xp (12GB each, Pascal, compute capability 6.1, no tensor cores), 32GB RAM, i5, Ubuntu. A secondary dev laptop (Windows, RTX 4060 8GB, Ada Lovelace) was also used and verified independently. Titan Xp latency is explicitly not a deployment claim — Pascal's lack of tensor cores and its specific memory-bandwidth/kernel-selection behavior means GPU latency does not always rank by parameter count or FLOPs (demonstrated directly in §4.3). Every latency number in this report additionally includes ONNX Runtime CPU latency for portability to hardware not owned by this project.

Latency protocol, applied identically everywhere in this report: single image, batch size 1, GPU synchronized (torch.cuda.synchronize()), warmed up, median and p95 reported over ≥200 runs. Median is reported as the headline number, not mean.

3.Experimental Roadmap

The sequence below is causal, not just chronological: each step was performed because the previous one produced a specific result that motivated it.

In prose: lightweight objective → Task A classification baseline → imbalance ablations → Task B region classification (which revealed the rare-class problem was partly a task-framing problem) → detection baseline → occlusion diagnosis (the recurring structural finding of the whole project) → tracking-by-detection attempts → centroid/offset instance segmentation → a four-run performance plateau → an architecture bug discovered by inspecting the model, not by more training → the deep-backbone fix → a box-then-mask architecture pivot → a test-data-contamination bug caught and fixed → cross-validation on fold1/fold2 → the official confirmatory run (0.8101 AP50_segm) → a project-wide priority realignment to accuracy-first → a COCO-pretrained ResNet-50 second backbone → ensembling and SAM2 → the current 0.8594 AP50_segm result (3.9-point gap to TAPIS) → generalization to a second dataset and an efficiency pass, both still pending.

6.6Tracking-by-Detection

This required multiple rounds; documented as a single throughline.

MotivationLatency headroom (∼22ms/frame, well under a 33ms real-time budget) plus raw intermediate video frames existing at ∼1fps between sparse annotations (e.g. CASE001: 10,972 raw files vs. 329 annotated) — checked directly before building anything.
Configuration v1IOU-Tracker (Bochinski et al. 2017, greedy box-IoU association) with track survival through max_age missed frames, windowed inference around each annotated frame.
Result v1Recall improved in every bucket: isolated 0.899→0.913, light 0.920→0.930, heavy 0.645→0.683 — moving heavy-occlusion recall outside the 0.606–0.666 band seen in every prior variant. A causal-only rerun (backward-looking window) gave identical numbers, confirming no look-ahead leak in the original windowed setup.
Configuration v2 (full COCO-style mAP)Full COCO-style mAP@50 needs a low score threshold for a real precision/recall curve, not just the fixed-threshold recall table above.
Result v2 — NEGATIVE, correctivemAP@50 got worse with tracking: 0.7899–0.7900 tracked vs. 0.8264 raw (∼3.7pt drop), while occlusion recall barely moved this time (heavy 0.645→0.652–0.655, far short of the earlier 0.683).
Root causeLow score threshold let far more low-confidence detections become track material; any unmatched-but-alive track coasted forward on its last box regardless of why it went unmatched. Two failure modes: noise-born tracks coasting as confident-looking false positives, and tracks from instruments that genuinely left frame still coasting on a stale box.
Fixesmin_confidence_to_coast=0.5 alone: mAP@50 = 0.7914 (barely moved — low-confidence coasting was not the dominant failure mode). Boundary-exit detection (drop a track immediately if it was moving off-frame when its gap started) combined with confidence gating: mAP@50 = 0.8034 — boundary-exit did almost all of the recovery, confirming the dominant failure mode was confident tracks going stale on real frame-exit.

7.3Architecture Bug Diagnosis

TriggerScoping what a serious segmentation capacity increase would look like. Inspecting models/segmenters/centroid_offset.py directly — rather than assuming "just needs to be bigger" — found a real, previously undiagnosed bug.
The bugreturned_layers=[1, 2] was chosen to get a cheap fine stride-4 FPN output, but mobilenet_backbone/IntermediateLayerGetter prunes the network to stop at the deepest requested stage. Requesting only shallow stages (stride 4 and 8, 24 and 40 channels) meant MobileNetV3-Large's entire deep half — up to its 960-channel final stage, where most of its actual semantic capacity lives — was never computed at all: not frozen, not present in the model's graph.
ConsequenceEvery one of the four plateaued runs was training a model that literally could not see deep features. Confirmed by direct gradient inspection: the deepest backbone layers had zero graph presence under the old returned_layers.
FixRegistered a second variant, returned_layers=[1, 3, 5] (stride 4/16/32 at 24/80/960 channels), standard FPN usage feeding shallow-through-deep inputs into a top-down pathway. Backbone grows ∼1.9M→∼5.0M params, full model 2.1M→5.88M — still far lighter than the 18.9M-param detector. The original registration is kept unchanged so its four existing runs stay reproducible.

8.Accuracy-First Extension

8.1Change in Project Objective

The priority changed mid-project After the four failed attempts closing §7.8 (none closing the gap to anywhere near the original 3-point target), direct guidance came back from the PhD students running the upstream research this project feeds into: accuracy is the top priority, lightweight is secondary, not simultaneous — get accuracy as high as possible first, ideally above TAPIS's published benchmark; only once that is solid, check generalization to a second dataset; only after that, look into making the winning approach lightweight (see Section 1). 9.Methodological Corrections and Lessons Learned

9.1Official-split-as-validation (a major correction to the project's methodology)

What exactly happened

Discovery trigger: a direct question about whether metrics were being measured on test or validation data.

What was found: the split-resolution code defined official as (train, test) — the true 5-case held-out test set. Every config in the repository at the time (26 files checked, zero exceptions) used split: official. This means every validation loss/metric logged during training, every early-stopping decision, every checkpoint-selection decision, and every headline metric reported across Task A, Task B, detection, tracking, and the first phase of segmentation was measured directly on the official 5-case test split — there was no held-out validation set distinct from test anywhere in the project's history up to that date.

Significance: this is not label leakage (test labels never entered the loss function). It is model-selection leakage: early stopping picks the checkpoint that scores best on test, and every comparative judgment made in that period (copy-paste vs. plain augmentation, the backbone-depth bug fix, the tracking max_age sweep) was made by watching test-set performance move. This inflates every reported number's credibility as a true generalization estimate, by an unquantified amount. It had not previously been flagged in the project instructions, the specification document, or any prior results writeup.

How it was caught: a direct question prompted checking the actual split-resolution source and every config file, rather than assuming the answer. There was no automated test or code-review step in place at the time that would have caught it.

What was done to correct it: the fix already existed and cost nothing to build — fold1/fold2 exactly partition the 8 official-train cases and were already wired to resolve to disjoint (train, val) pairs that never touch official test; this had existed since early in the project and was simply never used for model selection. Policy from this point forward: new development work uses fold1 or fold2; once a configuration is locked in, exactly one final confirmatory run with split: official reports the number that goes in this report.

Explicitly not applied retroactively: all results completed before this point stand as reported, now carrying this caveat rather than being silently retracted or re-run. Rationale: rerunning ∼20 completed ablations under a fold split would cost GPU time disproportionate to the value, since the qualitative conclusions (the occlusion-recall ceiling, the capacity-scaling result, tracking's net-negative mAP) are unlikely to flip on a 4-case-smaller training set. Two segmentation runs already in progress at the time were left running on official for the same reason.

Which results carry this caveat vs. which are clean:

  • Carry the caveat (developed/selected using official-as-validation, before the fix): all of Section 4 (Task A) and Section 5 (Task B); all of Section 6 (detection ablations and the tracking tuning sweep); the first four centroid/offset segmentation runs and the deep-backbone fix runs in §7.2–7.4.
  • Clean, post-fix: the Mask R-CNN pivot onward (§7.6 through Section 8) — fold1/fold2 used correctly as disjoint development splits (net of the separate, second leakage bug in §7.6, caught and fixed before producing a number), with each accuracy claim's official confirmatory run reported and explicitly distinguished from the fold-based development estimate.

9.2Test-data contamination in the first box-then-mask attempt

Covered in full in §7.6: the first attempt at warm-starting the Mask R-CNN on fold1 reused a box detector that had, unnoticed, already received gradient updates from fold1's own cases (because it was trained under split: official, which spans both folds). This gave a val mAP@50 of 0.9946 after a single epoch — an impossible number given as little training as one epoch, which is exactly what made it suspicious enough to investigate rather than accept. It was not caught by a held-out check; it was caught because the number itself was implausible. The run was killed immediately (∼2.5 minutes of GPU time) and never produced a value that appears anywhere in this report as a result.

9.3Repeated official-test evaluation during the accuracy-first push

Tension identified during this revision, not previously flagged in project documentation

The project's own stated discipline (§2.2, §9.1) is "exactly one locked confirmatory run on official test per finished configuration." The accuracy-first sequence in Section 8 ran six distinct evaluations directly against the official test set in one continuous session: COCO ResNet-50 alone, the two-way ensemble, the three-way ensemble, SAM2 zero-shot, SAM2 fine-tuned, and the four-way ensemble — with the decision to add each successive member made by observing the previous evaluation's official-test score.

This is the same category of problem as §9.1, at smaller scale: each "should we add this member" decision was informed by official-test feedback, not a held-out development estimate. It does not mean the 0.8594 number is wrong, and no step in the sequence showed the kind of implausible jump that flagged the §9.2 leak — but it means the ensemble's reported gains (+0.0349, +0.0072, +0.0041) should be read as this project's best current estimate under real inference-time conditions, not as a number with the same procedural guarantee as §7.8's single official run. This is now stated as an explicit limitation (Section 11) rather than left implicit.

9.4Corrections made during this document's preparation

Two factual errors in the project's prior internal framing were caught while assembling this report and are corrected here rather than silently carried forward:

Same-architecture fold1/fold2 ensemble comparison, corrected Prior framing (used in earlier project communication) described the fold1+fold2 same-architecture WBF ensemble as landing "below either fold's own single model." Checked directly against the raw evaluation log for this revision: the ensemble (0.7934) is actually higher than both fold1 alone (0.7652) and fold2 alone (0.7616). The comparison that appears to have been intended is that the fold-ensemble (0.7934) falls short of the single model trained on the complete 8-case official training set (0.8101) — not that ensembling made things worse than either input. Ensembling two independently-trained, disjoint-case models is a real, unsurprising gain over either alone; it just does not substitute for training one model on all the data at once. This report uses the corrected framing throughout (§7.8).
Three near-identical detection "baseline" numbers, disambiguated An earlier version of this report's prose cited 0.8295, 0.8264, and 0.831/0.8307 in different places without always naming which specific run each came from, reading as an unresolved inconsistency. Traced against manifests for this revision: 0.8295 is the original Adam, no-augmentation detection baseline (§6.1); 0.8264 is the copy-paste-trained detector (§6.5), which was subsequently adopted as the base detector for every tracking experiment (§6.6) and is therefore the correct "raw" comparison point in that section; 0.8307 (rounded to 0.831 in prose) is the class-weighted-loss detector (§6.3). All three are real, correct numbers for three different configurations; none was fabricated or in conflict, but they were not consistently labeled by name in earlier framing. This report names the specific configuration next to every one of these numbers.

9.5Other lessons recorded during the project

  • An intermittent-looking training crash coinciding with a known unrelated issue (a suspected network outage) is still worth checking against system logs before accepting the coincidence as the explanation — the real cause was a kernel OOM kill (§8.4).
  • A causally-plausible code fix (the non-causal-vs-causal tracking window check, §6.6) was verified empirically even though code inspection alone suggested it was already correct — it was, but the empirical check was still worth running rather than trusting inspection alone.
  • A result that looks like a clean win on one diagnostic (tracking's fixed-threshold occlusion recall) can be a net negative on mAP; both numbers are reported together throughout, not just the more favorable one.
  • An implausibly good number (near-100% after one epoch, §7.6) is itself diagnostic evidence of leakage and should be investigated before being reported, not treated as a lucky result.

11.Limitations

  1. Task B assumes ground-truth instance location (uses the true mask outline, not a predicted one) — it measures classification given localization, not full-pipeline accuracy.
  2. Rare instruments (Clip Applier: 102 instances total, 64 in the train split alone; Laparoscopic Grasper: 275) give low statistical confidence on their own metrics throughout every task.
  3. Only 13 real surgeries total (8 train / 5 test) — inter-surgery and inter-surgeon variation is not well captured by one split, official or fold-based.
  4. The occlusion-stratified recall metric used from §6.4 onward is a box-overlap proxy, not true pixel-level occlusion, and is evaluated at one fixed confidence threshold rather than swept the way AP is.
  5. Segmentation instance-level scores (AP50_segm) in the centroid/offset architecture (Section 7.1–7.5) were computed on a downsampled version of model output, not full input resolution — real mask quality on that architecture is likely underestimated.
  6. The box-then-mask model (§7.6) reuses the box detector's pretrained weights rather than training box localization from scratch in the reported configuration — box accuracy is inherited from Section 6, not independently re-verified at the segmentation stage (the from-scratch ablation in §7.8 tested this and found it did not help on official, but that is a different question from whether the inherited box quality itself has blind spots).
  7. No CPU/ONNX latency was measured for the detection or segmentation models specifically (only for Task A backbones) — deemed less reliable to estimate for two-stage detectors with a dynamic-length RPN/ROI pipeline; this number is reported as missing rather than estimated.
  8. Literature comparison numbers (TAPIS and related work) came from published papers and web search, not a formal systematic review.
  9. Every finding from Section 4 through the first four segmentation runs in §7.2 was selected using official-test-as-validation, a methodological error corrected partway through the project (Section 9.1) but not retroactively re-verified.
  10. The Section 8 four-model ensemble (0.8594) is explicitly not a lightweight result: ∼778ms/∼1.3fps per frame, roughly 23× too slow for real-time use. It should be read as the answer to "how good can accuracy get if cost is not yet a constraint," not a straight upgrade of the 0.8101 lightweight number (§8.7).
  11. Each ensemble configuration in Section 8 was evaluated once on the official test set, with each successive addition decided by observing that same test set (§9.3) — there is no repeated-run variance estimate for the ensemble deltas, and the exact per-model increments (+0.0349/+0.0072/+0.0041) should not be treated as guaranteed-reproducible.
  12. SAM2's decoder fine-tuning (§8.5) used only GraSP's official training cases and has not been checked for cross-dataset generalization — directly relevant given the project's still-pending generalizability validation step (Section 13).
  13. A full per-class AP50_segm breakdown for the fine-tuned SAM2 model and a raw numeric co-occurrence table (as opposed to the existing figure) were not available in the project's records at the time this report was assembled (Appendix C).

Best-supported findings

  1. Occlusion between co-occurring instruments, not class imbalance or model capacity, was the structural bottleneck of every Faster R-CNN-family detector tried — confirmed by six independent interventions (augmentation, loss weighting, backbone scaling, copy-paste, soft-NMS, tracking) all landing in the same 0.606–0.666 heavy-occlusion recall band (§6.4).
  2. Region classification with a lightweight model outperforms whole-frame multi-label classification with the heaviest model tried, on exactly the classes that whole-frame classification struggles with — the rare-class problem was partly a task-framing problem (Section 5).
  3. Architecture family, more than capacity, appears to have been the binding constraint for instance-level segmentation: a 5.88M-param model with the right architecture (box-then-mask) beat a fundamentally different but larger-capacity 2.1M→5.88M-param centroid/offset model by over 40 AP50_segm points (Section 7).
  4. Ensembling architecturally different models (different backbones, different pretraining regimes, or a structurally different foundation-model pipeline) produces real, additive gains at near-zero extra training cost; ensembling the same architecture on disjoint data subsets does not (Section 8).
  5. GPU latency ranking does not track parameter count on Pascal-generation hardware without tensor cores; ONNX CPU latency does — a project-specific but methodologically important reminder that benchmark hardware choice changes which model looks efficient (§4.3).

Open questions

  1. Does either the 0.8101 lightweight result or the 0.8594 ensemble result generalize to a second, independent surgical-instrument dataset? Neither has been tested outside GraSP.
  2. Is a fifth ensemble member worth pursuing, given diminishing returns (+0.0349 → +0.0072 → +0.0041) and the ensemble's growing latency cost, which now directly works against the pending efficiency phase?
  3. How much of the ensemble's reported 3.9-point gap would survive a properly held-out (rather than official-test-informed) member-selection process, per the §9.3 caveat?
  4. What does an efficiency pass (quantization, pruning, distillation, or a smaller SAM2 checkpoint targeting the 398ms encoder bottleneck) recover from either the lightweight or the accuracy-first result, once priorities 1 and 2 are satisfied?

This project is not finished. The accuracy-first priority (1 of 3, per Section 1) has a strong, honestly-measured result; generalizability (priority 2) has not been started against a second dataset; efficiency (priority 3) has not been started at all beyond the diagnostic latency measurement in §8.7.

13.Recommended Next Experiments

  1. Validate on a second dataset (priority 2). Test the 0.8101 lightweight result, the 0.8132 COCO-ResNet50 result, and the 0.8594 ensemble on a second, independent surgical-instrument dataset before treating any of them as "the" accuracy number for this approach. Candidate datasets have been researched but not yet acted on.
  2. Decide deliberately, not by default, whether to pursue a fifth ensemble member or a stronger backbone. Diminishing returns are already visible by the fourth member (§8.6); this should be a conscious cost/benefit call, not an assumed next step.
  3. Re-derive the ensemble build-up decision under a properly held-out protocol (fold1/fold2 rather than official) to produce a cleaner estimate of whether each addition would have been chosen without official-test feedback, addressing the §9.3 methodological tension directly rather than only disclosing it.
  4. Only after 1–3 are satisfied, begin the efficiency pass (priority 3): quantization, pruning, distillation, or a smaller SAM2 checkpoint, targeting first the 398ms SAM2 image-encoder bottleneck identified in §8.7, and evaluating parallel (rather than sequential) execution of the ensemble's four members across processes/GPUs.
  5. Measure the box-then-mask model's own speed/size independently (still not done, Limitation #7) — and consider retraining its box-drawing component from scratch as a check that the inherited box accuracy is not hiding a blind spot.
  6. One final combined accuracy-vs-cost chart spanning every model in this report (classification, detection, segmentation, single models and the ensemble) once the above are complete.

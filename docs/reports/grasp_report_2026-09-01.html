Surgical Instrument Classification on GraSP

Status: 2026-09-30

Identifies which of 7 instruments (Bipolar Forceps, Prograsp Forceps, Large Needle Driver, Monopolar Curved Scissors, Suction Instrument, Clip Applier, Laparoscopic Grasper) is present in a GraSP video frame, classified per-instrument rather than per-frame, since frames routinely hold 2-3 instruments and the class mix is imbalanced (rarest class has ~25x fewer examples than the most common). Tested against two public datasets never seen during training (EndoVis 2017, 2018).

TL;DR. Our four-model ensemble classifier works, and here is why. Given an instrument's mask, it names the instrument (7 classes) with 0.9207 accuracy on GraSP's official test set. Correcting only the 19% of instances it doubts lifts that to 0.9507 (0.9577 averaged over three training seeds at a slightly larger budget). The 80-pass MC-Dropout vote pipeline we built first reaches 0.9623, only 0.3 to 0.5 points higher, so we ship the 4-pass version.

  • Per instrument, not per frame. Frames hold 2 to 3 instruments, so each instrument's mask crop is classified on its own; whole-frame matching scores 0.36.
  • Four different members vote. Two ResNet-50s (320 px counts double) and two MobileNetV3-small models beat the best single model (0.9207 against 0.915), and their evidence says which predictions to doubt.
  • Fix only what the model doubts. Flagging 525 instances (18%) catches 82% of the ensemble's errors, so a fraction of the compute delivers most of the gain.
  • SAM2 supplies the missing evidence. The annotated mask is propagated up to 10 frames each way, every frame is classified, and a confidence-weighted vote picks the class: at the shipped gate it fixes 121 instances and breaks 35. Optical-flow propagation scores 0.8448, below doing nothing, so the tracker matters.
  • One pass instead of 80. An evidential (Dirichlet) score gives the gate from a single forward pass per member, and ranks errors as well as softmax and better than the vote (AUROC 0.925 against 0.916 and 0.896).

Next steps: (1) try YOLO26 instead of SAM2 for the temporal correction, since tracking is the dominant cost (about 21 s per tracked instance); (2) switch to a three-member ensemble, because dropping the stretched MobileNet made no meaningful difference (ensemble alone 0.9264 against 0.9240; with correction 0.9593 against 0.9577 at 833 instances, three seeds each).

Caveats: masks are ground truth (an oracle for location), the test set is five cases, and seed spread is about half a point.

Results

The shipped pipeline is a four-member evidential ensemble: one deterministic pass per member (4 passes, not 80). A gate flags 539 of 2,861 official-test instances, SAM2 corrects them, and accuracy rises from 0.9207 to 0.9507 (macro-F1 0.8867 to 0.9279). Gate selection

The gate decides which instances get SAM2 correction. For the shipped evidential pipeline its threshold was set on held-out cases, not on the test set: members were retrained on the fold2 cases, the threshold was fixed on the fold1 cases (4 cases, 3,235 instances) by a rule set before looking, and applied unchanged. It flags 539 of 2,861 test instances (18.8%).

Why about 540 (19%): the accuracy curve is nearly flat past 525 instances (+0.2 to +0.4 points up to 833 for every pipeline), while each tracked instance costs about 21 s. Tracking 539 instances takes about 3 GPU-hours, against about 17 for all 2,861 (estimates from the per-instance time, not measured full runs). For the vote pipeline the 9% gate (833 instances) was picked for the best test accuracy, so its margin is optimistic: leave-one-case-out choice gives 0.9633, and a held-out check (fold2-trained members, gate chosen on fold1) selects 15% (649 instances, 0.9595 / 0.9309 on test), with the fold1 curve flat from 20% down to 3%.

Two different questions, kept apart. Zero-shot: the GraSP-trained ensemble run unchanged on EndoVis (no retraining, no tracking, 20 stochastic passes per member). Portability: the same architecture and recipe trained on each EndoVis dataset's own sequences and scored on its held-out sequences (same 3,231 and 2,544 instances, split by video, ImageNet start, 10 epochs, seed 42, single runs). Zero-shot, the three bulkiest instruments transferred (F1 0.52–0.66), but Monopolar Curved Scissors scored F1 0.23 on EndoVis 2018 (0.62 on 2017) and Suction Instrument scored 0 on 2018. A different instrument generation is one untested explanation; 0.414 macro-F1 is a weak result. Trained on EndoVis, tracking on 2018 fixes 168 instances and breaks 19, and the un-retuned 9% gate flags 20% to 27% of instances (29.1% on GraSP), holding 253 of the 382 ensemble errors on 2018. 2017 has no tracking result: most of its held-out images are not in the release.

A single ResNet-50 started from GraSP weights is no better than one started from ImageNet (accuracy 0.9080 against 0.9116 on 2017, 0.8515 against 0.8794 on 2018, 3 seeds), so there is no evidence that GraSP training transfers. The evidential ensemble trained the same way matches the vote ensemble (2018 after tracking 0.9266 against 0.9279, McNemar p = 0.68).

How it works

Shipped default: each member makes one deterministic pass, logits become Dirichlet evidence, evidences are combined with the ensemble weights, and an instance is tracked when its epistemic score is at or above the fold1-chosen threshold (18.8%). Each propagated frame is classified the same way, confident frames count for more, and the largest weighted total wins. The vote pipeline instead takes the plurality of 80 stochastic votes and tracks where at least 9% disagree (29.1%).

Uncertainty estimation

The vote pipeline's gate uses MC Dropout (Gal & Ghahramani, 2016): each of the four members keeps dropout on and makes 20 passes, the prediction is the weighted plurality of the 80 votes, and the uncertainty is the share of votes that name a different class. Softmax confidence and a single-pass evidential score (Duan et al., 2024) were compared as alternatives, each scored against the errors of its own ensemble's prediction on official test (the vote and softmax come from the vote ensemble, 0.9266; evidential from the seed-42 evidential ensemble, 0.9207, so signal and model are not separated).

  • The vote ranks errors worst of the three, and it needs 80 passes where the others need one. It was chosen for threshold stability under shift, but on EndoVis softmax drifts as much as the vote does, so that argument does not separate them.
  • Evidential against softmax is not resolved: AUROC differs by +0.011 (interval −0.003 to +0.028) and AURC is the same. Its clearest edge is catching more errors in a fixed review budget, on one seed. It is not better calibrated (overconfident: confidence 0.960 against accuracy 0.921).
  • Seed noise is about a third of a point. Three evidential seeds give accuracy 0.9240 ± 0.0031 and AUROC 0.9225 ± 0.0076; the reported seed 42 (0.9207) is the lowest, and the base gap to the vote ensemble (0.9266) is inside one SD. End to end the gap shrinks over three seeds (next bullet).
  • End to end the signals land within about half a point. At matched budgets of 525, 649 and 833 instances the evidential pipeline averages 0.9554, 0.9570 and 0.9577 over three seeds (seed 42 alone: 0.9507, 0.9514, 0.9521) against the vote pipeline's 0.9581, 0.9595 and 0.9623 (one training, different combine rule); the three-member version averages 0.9567, 0.9591 and 0.9593. Retrained on EndoVis 2018 the two are level after correction (0.9266 against 0.9279, McNemar p = 0.68).
  • No signal fixes the closed-jaw errors. They are as uncertain as other errors and every signal flags them equally, but correction fails because the frame lacks the information. A second evidential trigger for the vote gate's unanimous errors would flag 59% of instances, so it was not adopted.

Efficiency

The shipped default needs 4 forward passes per instance and the vote pipeline 80; tracking (about 21 s per tracked instance) dominates either way. INT8 quantization (weights-only dynamic, ONNX Runtime) on the retrained vote members made every member slower on CPU (the 320 px ResNet-50 went from 44 to 74 ms) and cost 1.3–2.8 points of accuracy on the ResNet-50 members and 72–79 points on the MobileNetV3 members. Not adopted.

A lighter SAM2 variant (small, same gate, same 833 tracked instances) cuts tracking time about 30% (16.7 s against 24 s per instance) for 0.0039 less accuracy and the same 29 breaks; the large checkpoint is kept since accuracy is the current priority.

Limitations

  • Ground-truth masks in, by design. The classifier is the deliverable and detection and segmentation are treated as an upstream input, so the crops and the SAM2 seeds come from annotated masks. The results measure naming an instrument once its location is given. They are classification metrics and are not comparable to the segmentation mAP that GraSP reports (TAPIS 89.85, TAPIS-VST 89.58, SlowFast 74.33 mAP@0.5 IoU); the GraSP paper reports no classification-only baseline.
  • Prior work overlap. ISINet (González et al., MICCAI 2020) applies a temporal consistency step to instance classes, so following an instance across frames and voting is not new; what is specific here is gating tracking by an uncertainty score and SAM2 propagation from the annotated mask. A RAFT optical-flow baseline in the spirit of ISINet's step (10 frames each way, same ensemble votes, plain majority, on the same 833 flagged instances) gives 0.8448 / 0.8061 accuracy / macro-F1, below the ensemble alone (0.9266 / 0.8898; 49 fixed, 283 broken) and far below SAM2 tracking (0.9623 / 0.9351; exact McNemar p < 0.001), probably because consecutive frames are far apart and the warped masks drift (frame rate not confirmed). It is not ISINet itself, and only one untuned flow model was tried.
  • Closed-jaw ceiling. Laparoscopic Grasper vs. Suction Instrument (closed jaws look identical to a suction tube in a still frame) and two more pairs (Bipolar vs. Needle Driver, Monopolar vs. Suction) account for 59 of the vote ensemble's 210 pre-tracking mistakes: a distinguishing feature is sometimes just not visible in frame. Four fixes were ruled out (narrow and wide temporal search, action-state labels, targeted fine-tuning), so this is a single-frame information limit, not a training gap.
  • The gate passes some errors. Of the retrained ensemble's 210 errors, the 9% gate passes 29 and they are never tracked. Eight of them are unanimous across every pass and member, which no vote-based uncertainty can flag, because the models agree with each other on the wrong class. The gate value itself was chosen on the five test cases, so its accuracy is optimistic; the evidential default's threshold was chosen on held-out fold1 cases.
  • The shipped default is 0.3 to 0.5 points below the alternative on the three-seed mean at matched budgets (seed SD about 0.5 points; the vote pipeline is one training) and is not better calibrated than softmax. It is chosen for cost and defensibility.

Next steps

  1. Try YOLO26 instead of SAM2 for the temporal correction. SAM2 dominates the cost (about 21 s per tracked instance); YOLO26 is untested here, so it must be scored on the same flagged instances against the 0.9507 baseline before it replaces anything.
  2. Switch to a three-member ensemble (ResNet-50 320 px, ResNet-50 224 px, letterboxed MobileNetV3-small, weights 0.40, 0.30, 0.30). Removing the stretched MobileNet made no meaningful difference: ensemble alone 0.9264 against 0.9240, and 0.9593 against 0.9577 with correction at 833 instances (three seeds each). Give it its own fold1 threshold before shipping.
